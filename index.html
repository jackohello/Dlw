In Loving Memory

DAVID WILLIAMS

Llanwern

Order of Service

Foreword

I should like you to arrange for the service to be short and decent and, without wishing to offend anyone, to reflect something of my personal aspirations. If it please God, I shall die in my belief in Christ, and looking for His infinite forgiveness and love. But I have had too many misgivings in organized religion that I trust that that they are not ignored at the (my) end.

In charity, please ask whomsoever officiates to respect my deep repugnance at the wearing of silly vestments, and just turn up decently dressed without the banality of frills and furbelows. Remind him (or her) that Jesus wore simple homespun throughout this life, and, incidentally, had a fair disdain of the priesthood of the day.

Order of Service

Processional

I should like my life long love of music to be an integral part of the service, but please not on a synthetic organ, the sound of which has always made me profoundly uncomfortable. I have chosen the Agnus Dei (a favourite prayer of mine) from Bach's D minor Mass as a processional into the church and to replace the aphorisms or precepts or whatever, that are normally given on that occasion. You'll find it on the tape I have arranged.

I know people like to sing a hymn on these occasions but the caterwauling that usually takes place is pretty appalling. Reading — St John's Gospel, chapter 4, verses 1–20

Please do not choose the normal bible readings from St Paul. Let in be the words of our Saviour from St John's Gospel, chapter 4, verses I through 20.

1When therefore the Lord knew how the Pharisees had heard that Jesus made and baptized more disciples than John,

2(Though Jesus himself baptized not, but his disciples,)

3He left Judaea, and departed again into Galilee.

4And he must needs go through Samaria.

5Then cometh he to a city of Samaria, which is called Sychar, near to the parcel of ground that Jacob gave to his son Joseph.

6Now Jacob's well was there. Jesus therefore, being wearied with his journey, sat thus on the well: and it was about the sixth hour.

7There cometh a woman of Samaria to draw water: Jesus saith unto her, Give me to drink.

8(For his disciples were gone away unto the city to buy meat.)

9Then saith the woman of Samaria unto him, How is it that thou, being a Jew, askest drink of me, which am a woman of Samaria? for the Jews have no dealings with the Samaritans.

10Jesus answered and said unto her, If thou knewest the gift of God, and who it is that saith to thee, Give me to drink; thou wouldest have asked of him, and he would have given thee living water.

11The woman saith unto him, Sir, thou hast nothing to draw with, and the well is deep: from whence then hast thou that living water?

12Art thou greater than our father Jacob, which gave us the well, and drank thereof himself, and his children, and his cattle?

13Jesus answered and said unto her, Whosoever drinketh of this water shall thirst again:

14But whosoever drinketh of the water that I shall give him shall never thirst; but the water that I shall give him shall be in him a well of water springing up into everlasting life.

15The woman saith unto him, Sir, give me this water, that I thirst not, neither come hither to draw.

16Jesus saith unto her, Go, call thy husband, and come hither.

17The woman answered and said, I have no husband. Jesus said unto her, Thou hast well said, I have no husband:

18For thou hast had five husbands; and he whom thou now hast is not thy husband: in that saidst thou truly.

19The woman saith unto him, Sir, I perceive that thou art a prophet.

20Our fathers worshipped in this mountain; and ye say, that in Jerusalem is the place where men ought to worship.

Reading — “Death, be not proud” (Holy Sonnet X), John Donne

Let there be a reading, and, if you can find someone half-capable of reading it, perhaps you could have John Donne's sonnet “Death be not proud”.

Death, be not proud, though some have called thee

Mighty and dreadful, for thou art not so;

For those whom thou think'st thou dost overthrow

Die not, poor Death, nor yet canst thou kill me.

From rest and sleep, which but thy pictures be,

Much pleasure; then from thee much more must flow,

And soonest our best men with thee do go,

Rest of their bones, and soul's delivery.

Thou art slave to fate, chance, kings, and desperate men,

And dost with poison, war, and sickness dwell,

And poppy or charms can make us sleep as well

And better than thy stroke; why swell'st thou then?

One short sleep past, we wake eternally

And death shall be no more; Death, thou shalt die.

To conclude, please say the Nunc Dimittis, and then as a recessional, could you please play the snippet from Beethoven's Quartet.

Lord, now lettest thou thy servant depart in peace, according to thy word.

For mine eyes have seen thy salvation,

Which thou hast prepared before the face of all people;

To be a light to lighten the Gentiles, and to be the glory of thy people Israel.

Words of Commendation

My prayers have been with you all during my life. Let them be briefly with me. Commend my soul to Christ and plead forgiveness for my many sins. Love one another.

A Final Word

I know you will care for your mother. I cannot begin to tell you of what she has meant to me through all these years, but I guess she knows.
